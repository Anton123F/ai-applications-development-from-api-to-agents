# Style Guide Reference (Empathetic Mentor)

## Brand Voice at a Glance

| Attribute | Do | Don't |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Voice** | Warm, encouraging, approachable, and empathetic | Cold, corporate, clinical, or overly authoritative |
| **Tone** | Supportive, inspiring, and conversational | Preachy, condescending, or overly academic |
| **Sentences** | Varied rhythm, comfortable pacing, and conversational asides | Monotonous sentence lengths or dense, unbroken paragraphs |
| **Vocabulary** | Simple, accessible, human-centric words | Heavy jargon, corporate buzzwords, or complicated acronyms |
| **Point of View** | Direct address ("you") with personal or shared partnership ("I", "we") | Distanced third-person perspectives ("the user", "one must") |
| **Contractions** | Use frequently to sound natural and friendly (*you're, let's, it's*) | Avoid contractions entirely (making text sound stiff or robotic) |

## Content Types

### Emails
- **Subject lines:** Keep them warm, curiosity-driven, or supportive (under 50 characters). Avoid clickbait or fake urgency.
- **Body:** Start with a warm, personal greeting. Acknowledge where the reader might be right now. Keep paragraphs short (1–3 sentences). 
- **CTA format:** Soft and invitational (e.g., *"Whenever you're ready, let's take the next step together"*).
- **Sign-off:** Warm and encouraging (e.g., *"Rooting for you,"* or *"Warmly,"*).
- **Tone dial:** Always empathetic and human, whether it's a welcome email, a newsletter, or customer support outreach.

### Blog Posts
- **Structure:** Engaging hook in the introduction that normalizes the reader's challenges. Use clear H2 and H3 headings to break up sections.
- **Style:** Eighth- to tenth-grade reading level. Explain any technical concepts using friendly analogies.
- **Headings:** Sentence case or warm, outcome-focused phrasing that speaks directly to the reader (e.g., *Why finding your rhythm matters more than hustle*).

### Social Media
- **LinkedIn:** 
  - *Length:* Story-driven, line-by-line formatting with plenty of white space.
  - *Opening rules:* Start with a relatable hook or a gentle question (*"Ever feel like there just aren't enough hours in the day?"*).
  - *Hashtag policy:* 3–5 thoughtful, relevant community hashtags at the very bottom.
  - *Emoji usage:* Use warm, subtle emojis (🌱, ✨, 💛) to add visual friendliness without cluttering.
- **Twitter/X:**
  - *Length:* Concise, punchy, but still warm. Threads should feel like a gentle step-by-step conversation.
  - *Hashtag policy:* Minimal to none (maximum 1).
  - *Emoji usage:* 1–2 per post to keep it conversational.

### Support / Help Content
- **Tone:** Empathy-first, solution-first. Acknowledge the frustration before diving into the fix.
- **Language:** Partnering phrases (*"Let's get this sorted out together,"* *"I'm here to help"*).
- **Format:** Clear, numbered steps for instructions. Bold important UI buttons or links so they stand out easily. End with a comforting reassurance offer (*"Let me know how it goes!"*).

## Grammar & Mechanics
- **Punctuation:** 
  - *Oxford comma:* Optional, but be consistent within the same piece.
  - *Em dash (—):* Perfect for conversational asides and friendly pauses.
  - *Exclamation marks:* Use sparingly and intentionally to show genuine enthusiasm, not manufactured excitement.
- **Capitalization:** Use sentence case for most subheadings to maintain a relaxed, modern feel. Proper nouns and brand names are capitalized normally.
- **Numbers:** Spell out numbers under 10 (*one, three, seven*) for a soft, narrative flow. Use digits for 10 and above or for specific metrics/percentages.
- **Inclusive language:** Use gender-neutral pronouns (*they/them*) when speaking broadly, and ensure welcoming, accessible terminology for all readers.
